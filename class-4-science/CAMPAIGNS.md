# Three hands-on science campaigns

Research and implementation: 21 September 2026. Scope: the three topic groups specifically requested; the other 17 Science games retain their previous gameplay. Maths files are outside this update.

## Textbook grounding and difficulty

Primary sources are NCERT *Our Wondrous World*, Grade 4, first edition March 2025, 2026–27 reprint:

- [How Things Work](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/deev107.pdf), pp. 104–116: making and exploring spinners, changing their construction, predicting and observing. The teacher guidance emphasises exploration rather than formal theory. Balance, centre of mass, rolling and coasting are qualitative extensions requested by the user; no torque or inertia formula is required.
- [How Things Are Made](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/deev108.pdf), pp. 117–127: tear used paper, soak with a small amount of methi as a binder, mash, spread/roll a thin sheet and dry; resource conservation and the five Rs. The game separates preparation, pulp formation, spreading, pressing and drying into visibly different actions. Its water counts and drying time are game units, not a physical recipe.
- [Our Sky](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/deev110.pdf), pp. 152–163: observing changing shadows and Sun positions, torch/object investigations and shadow puppetry. The games change source distance, object distance, height and screen position separately before combining them.

The official PDFs were read from downloaded copies. This is original curriculum-linked material, not an official NCERT product. Content is not a verbatim textbook answer key.

## What changes between successive levels

Each campaign has ten authored problems, ends at level ten and allows explicit replay. There is no automatic wraparound with different colours or numbers presented as a new level. A replay intentionally repeats a known problem and cannot farm XP. The three campaigns remain three games, within the maximum five-game-per-topic requirement.

| Level | Spin & Balance Carnival | Shadow Theatre Adventure | Paper Rescue Factory |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Drag a lighter crate along a beam to counterbalance a fixed heavy crate | Move the light vertically to aim a shadow in the opposite direction | Sort used material into a classroom paper batch or other materials |
| 2 | Swipe or set a launch strength to land in a speed target | Move a lamp towards a dragon cut-out to enlarge its silhouette | Tear a sheet across and down into smaller pieces |
| 3 | Arrange two movable masses around a two-dimensional carousel | Walk a robot cut-out towards a screen to shrink its shadow | Control a water valve to soften scraps without overflowing |
| 4 | Find the support point of an irregular plate | Lift a cat puppet without changing its distance from the screen | Stir around a bowl to turn softened pieces into pulp |
| 5 | Reorder three tower blocks before a gust tests stability | Aim two lamps to place two partially illuminated shadow regions | Transfer a fixed amount of pulp from thick patches to thin patches |
| 6 | Capture slow and fast colour-wheel observations | Record three times of day on a model sundial | Roll at suitable pressure across the whole sheet |
| 7 | Move paired weights outward to change coasting under stated model conditions | Black out the light during a scene change to cross a forbidden stage area | Change rack spacing, airflow and rain protection before drying |
| 8 | Choose a wheel shape that delivers a fragile parcel smoothly | Draw a rectangular cut-out to make a target-sized enlarged shadow | Fit three useful shapes onto the clear parts of used paper |
| 9 | Draw a uniform rotor plate; its measured centroid controls the balance test | Move the screen and aim the lamp to fit a new target | Connect spatially arranged factory stations into a working process |
| 10 | Combine mass balance, stem position and launch speed for a final top | Perform three different theatre cues using source, puppet and screen | Fulfil an order using reusable sheets and one water-limited recycling batch |

## Feedback and accessible interaction

Trials animate the changed model. Failure leaves the problem open for revision with an explanation. Drag/draw actions have sliders, shape tools or button alternatives. Doodle is used for geometry that affects the result: a uniform rotor's area centroid and a rectangular cut-out's dimensions. The surrounding game shell, colours, typography and article structure remain consistent with the Maths package.

## Model boundaries

- Balance uses idealised mass and lever distances. Irregular-plate support is the centroid of equal-area cells. Gusts and bounce are exaggerated for visible feedback.
- Coasting compares equal starting angular speed and the same model resisting torque. It is not a universal claim that moving weights outward always makes any toy spin longer.
- Shadow projection uses a small point source and a flat object/screen. Multiple lights can partly illuminate shadow regions. The Sun path is idealised; noon is not overhead everywhere.
- Paper is a process model with conserved scoops and a limited water budget, not a manufacturing simulation. Real paper needs time to soften and dry. Reuse and recycling are distinct.
- The ten levels per campaign are finite authored content. Variety means different goals, manipulations, scenes and constraints; it does not promise infinitely unique science ideas or proven learning gains.

## Progress compatibility

Old records for the three replaced games are archived under previousEditions in the existing Science save key. Old XP is retained. New campaign progress starts at level one. Restart resets only the selected current campaign; completed-level replay awards no XP. Remaining Science records and all Maths content are unaffected.
